## Overview

During the first month of my internship, I reviewed how two departments planned, executed and reported their work: **Procurement**, and **Operations & Business Research**. I documented the operational gaps I found and proposed a low-cost, low-disruption framework to close them. The findings and recommendations were presented to management.

## The Problem

- **No predefined daily tasks** in either department. Work was not planned in advance, so output and productivity were hard to track.
- **Research tasks resembled academic assignments** rather than real business deliverables.
- **The operations function was neglected.** Activity was concentrated almost entirely on research, so one half of the department's mandate was not being fulfilled.
- **No standard way to document or report completed work** to management.

## Approach

1. **Elicitation:** direct observation of day-to-day task assignment, execution and reporting. I observed Procurement as its Team Lead, and Operations & Business Research as a team member.
2. **Validation:** findings were confirmed in meetings with management.
3. **Current-state mapping:** documented the "As-Is" process (below).
4. **Future-state design:** built the "To-Be" process in BPMN notation.

## Current "As-Is" Process

Tasks are assigned verbally with no written schedule, work is submitted by e-mail or Google Form, outcomes are discussed in meetings with no fixed agenda, and the cycle closes with no documentation trail.

![As-Is process map](diagrams/as-is-process.png)

## Proposed Solutions

| Identified Problem | Proposed Solution |
|---|---|
| No predefined daily tasks | Pre-mapped daily/weekly tasks for each team member, visible on the staff portal |
| Research tasks feel academic, not like real business work | Tasks reframed as genuine business deliverables tied to daily/weekly planning |
| Operations function neglected | Operations made fully functional, with interns oriented on daily execution and reporting |
| Narrow exposure to only one function | Weekly rotation of interns between Operations and Business Research |
| No structured documentation of work | Interns trained to document tasks for their portfolio and the organisational record |
| No standard reporting to management | Team leads submit written reports before every review meeting |

## Expected Benefits

- **For the company:** clearer visibility into daily output, better-documented work for future analysis, and a fully functional operations arm.
- **For interns:** defined daily direction, exposure to both operations and research, and a documented portfolio of real business work.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. Management review and approval of the task-mapping structure for both departments.
2. Confirmation of the staff portal as the central channel for task visibility.
3. A short orientation session for interns on the operations workflow and documentation standards.
4. Definition of the intern rotation schedule.
5. Agreement on the format and submission timeline for team leads' written reports.
